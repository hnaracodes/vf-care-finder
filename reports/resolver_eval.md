## How the cases were made

Thresholds were set before the first run: fuzzy score 85, ambiguity margin 3 points, outside-India distance 50 km.

Scoring: `ok` cases hit when the resolved state and district are one of the expected ones (and the kind matches, when one is given); `ambiguous` cases hit when every expected district is among the candidates; `not_found` and `outside_india` cases hit when the status matches.

Changes made after the first run (the cases file was not edited):

1. Care words (`hospital`, `clinic`, `nursing home`, `me`, `my`, `here`, `nearby`, `around`) are dropped from the place text with the other filler words. Fixes 54.
2. Cross-tier check: when the only exact match is a post office name, district and city names with a spelling score of at least 90 (no containment credit) in other districts compete with it, and the answer is `ambiguous`. Turns 27 from a silent wrong pick into an ambiguous answer.
3. Tried and reverted: matching the literal name before old names. It broke `Gurgaon` and `Allahabad`, which are also village office names (in Ranchi, Bareilly, Budaun; and in Patan, Gujarat), so a known rename of a district now competes in the district step, as first written.
4. PINs whose offices all have state `NA` (90 geocoded PINs) were missing from the first gazetteer; they now take the district of their 5 nearest offices. Found by the every-PIN check, not by a case.

Fix round after an independent review (Nov 21). The cases file was not edited; the frozen score is the same before and after (54 of 55, the one miss is still case 27). Changes:

1. Gazetteer: an office that is both `coord_placeholder` and `coord_suspect` is treated as suspect (its point was being used, which put rows such as Raj Garh Ajmer in Mizoram). A PIN is placed only by offices within 200 km of its main district's centre. An office placed by a PIN that is more than 200 km from the office's listed district takes the PIN's district. An office's precision is at least its distance from its PIN's point.
2. Gazetteer and resolver: India's outline (Natural Earth, public domain) replaces the bounding box as the test of "inside India". Office points more than 3 km outside it are treated as not geocoded, computed points that fall outside are moved to the nearest office point of their district, and typed coordinates outside it are `outside_india` (Lahore, Rajshahi, Sylhet and Biratnagar were `ok` before).
3. Resolver parsing: the whole text is matched exactly before a state or filler word is taken off (New Delhi, North Goa, East District); a trailing state is not taken off when only a bare word such as North or New would remain; a state after the last comma is parsed before a district ("Singhwara, Darbhanga, Bihar").
4. Resolver matching: a fuzzy district or city hit that only contains a name and leaves other words of the text unexplained no longer wins; a close spelling of an office competes, and if nothing explains the whole text the answer is a choice. A fuzzy match found only outside the state the user gave or typed is a choice, not `ok`.
5. Islands: the 200 km `coord_suspect` rule no longer applies in Lakshadweep and Andaman and Nicobar (Minicoy is about 300 km from the other Lakshadweep islands and was being moved to the district centre), and the 50 km distance-from-any-office rule for typed points now applies only north of 32.5 degrees, where the outline includes land India does not administer (it rejected Kavaratti and Minicoy).

## Limits

- A district answer is the district's centre, not the user's location; the label says "centre of X district, about N km across" (N is twice the 75th percentile office distance).
- Precision is a spread of post office points, not a measured error. Branch office coordinates in the directory are often wrong; suspect, placeholder, outside-India and outlier points are kept out of PIN centres but a row may still rest on a single office's point (said in `note`). A PIN placed by only two offices far apart keeps both, so its point is between them and its precision is wide.
- District centres are not trimmed. 31 of 754 district centres are more than 30 km from the median of their own PIN points and 13 more than 50 km (Sheopur 128 km, Kabirdham 121 km, Sahebganj 98 km, Kushi Nagar 82 km). The brief's remote districts: Sheopur 128 km from them, precision 159 km; Pakur 42 km from them, precision 64 km. The centre comes from head and sub office points (vf.geo.group_centres), and the same centres drive the 200 km `coord_suspect` rule, so changing them is a separate round.
- The outside-India check uses Natural Earth's 1:10m India outline (India's own view of its borders, public domain), simplified to about 0.5 km, with 3 km of slack for coasts and border towns. A foreign point within 3 km of the border is accepted. The outline includes Pakistan-administered Kashmir and Aksai Chin; north of 32.5 degrees a point inside it is rejected only if it is more than 50 km from every Indian post office point (see Muzaffarabad and Gilgit above: Muzaffarabad, 36 km from one, is accepted). South of that line the distance rule is off, so a point inside India far from any usable office point (Kavaratti, Minicoy) is accepted and given the district of its nearest PIN points.
- Island districts (Lakshadweep, Andaman and Nicobar) are exempt from the 200 km `coord_suspect` rule, because they span more than 200 km of sea; their points are checked against the outline only.
- A fuzzy match that explains only part of the text, or one found only outside the state the user gave, comes back as a choice (`ambiguous`, sometimes with one candidate), not as `ok`. This costs the user a click on queries such as "Lucknow Hazratganj Market".
- Old names come only from the fixed alias list in `vf.geo.CITY_ALIASES`; an old name not in it is matched by spelling or not at all.
- Only one person's expectations were scored. There are no real user queries.
